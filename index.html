The Attorney-Client Privilege in Breach Response: What Counsel Protects

When organizations suffer a data breach, the instinct is to act immediately — engage forensics, notify regulators, communicate to customers. But speed without structure can waive protections that are nearly impossible to restore. Retaining counsel first ensures that forensic work, root-cause analysis, and internal findings are conducted under privilege, shielded from discovery in subsequent litigation or regulatory proceedings. The distinction between privileged and non-privileged investigation work is not automatic — it must be deliberately established from the outset of the engagement.

02 February 2025

California's Expanded Breach Notification Obligations Under SB 1386 Amendments

California's breach notification framework has grown more demanding with successive amendments. Organizations operating in the state must notify affected residents without unreasonable delay — typically within 30 days of discovery — when defined categories of personal information are compromised. Notification content requirements are now prescriptive, mandating specific language about what occurred, what data was affected, and what remediation steps are available. Counsel should be engaged before any notification is drafted to ensure content, timing, and delivery method satisfy both statute and applicable regulatory expectations.

03 December 2024

Ransomware Response: Legal Considerations Before You Engage the Threat Actor

Ransomware incidents present a distinct legal layer that many organizations overlook in the urgency of recovery. Before any payment is considered — or before engaging directly with a threat actor — counsel must assess OFAC sanctions exposure, since certain ransomware operators are designated entities whose payment constitutes a federal violation regardless of intent. Beyond sanctions, organizations must evaluate notification obligations triggered by the event itself, separate from any decision to pay. Documented legal review of these questions, conducted under privilege, is essential to demonstrating a defensible, good-faith response posture.
